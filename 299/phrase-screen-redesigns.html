2 · Everything within thumb reach at the bottom

one-handed
  1. 1Grid stays visible while typing
  2. 2Speak + predictions by the thumb

The screen is flipped: vocabulary on top, and the message plus Speak docked right above the keyboard, like a chat app.

  • Fixes the "grid disappears while typing" problem seen on the Pixel.
  • Short reach for Speak and predictions; a familiar pattern from messaging apps.
  • Moves everything users already know, and the grid is still small while typing.

3 · Category rail

bold · fits #291
  1. 1All categories visible
  2. 2Explicit edit mode

Categories go in a vertical rail with large, stable targets. Selection is shown with a checkmark-style bar plus a fill, not just a color. Renaming, reordering and deleting move into an Edit mode, so a second tap no longer opens a menu by accident.

  • Scales to many categories without horizontal scrolling.
  • Removes the tap-twice-to-edit trap.
  • Needs a separate portrait fallback (drawer or wrapped chips).

4 · The message as tokens

bold · needs a spike
  1. 1Phrases = tokens, typing = plain text
  2. 2Language: badge + dashed line, not color only

The field draws the actual MessageParts. Phrase parts are tokens you can act on as a whole. Typed text stays editable as normal text in between.

  • One tap changes the language of a phrase, with no fiddly text selection.
  • Deleting a whole phrase is one tap; this matches the shared-session model (#258).
  • A custom text field with mixed tokens and text, cursor handling and IME support is real work.

5 · Two modes: Quick talk and Compose

bold · clearest mental model
  1. 1Tap tile = speak now
  2. 2Large, simple mode switch

Quick talk is a full-screen grid where a tap speaks straight away, with a strip showing what was just said and a repeat button. Compose is the typing workspace. This turns the hidden Immediate/Deferred SpeechPolicy setting into a visible mode.

  • Each mode is uncluttered; Quick talk gets the biggest targets of any design.
  • Matches two real situations: quick back-and-forth vs. building a message.
  • Mixing phrases and typing in one sentence means switching modes. "Hold thought" has to work across both.
